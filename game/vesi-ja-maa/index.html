How the quarter resolves

  • Recharge — meltwater in spring, almost nothing in midwinter. Summer draw runs hot while the aquifer is at its lowest.
  • Permits — an abstraction licence takes two quarters. File it while the aquifer is under 42 and the regional office refuses, and the council remembers.
  • Closed loop — cuts a site's draw to roughly a fifth. Tenants pay 15% less for it. Take the haircut early or pay for it in aquifer.
  • The oven — a heat exchanger sells waste heat into the district network. Worthless without a tenant, lucrative in January, and the single fastest way back into the council's good books.
  • The analyst — a processor like any other, running at priority 75 on the same state the council and the aquifer see. It projects forward with the world's own arithmetic, names the binding constraint, and writes the verdict back into the tick. Scrub its strip to read what it said at the time, not what it would say now.
  • Forking — every tick is kept. Scrub back on the rail and fork: the same weather, the same buyers, your decisions rewritten. The analyst is deterministic, so when two branches disagree, the disagreement is yours. Three forks.

Modelled as an Archetype world — components on entities, processors in priority order, every tick persisted as immutable history, forks as first-class counterfactuals. The analyst is a component, not an overlay: its judgement is world state and forks with everything else.
Municipalities are real; parcels, tenants and hydrology are invented.
